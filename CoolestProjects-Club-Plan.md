# Coolest Projects — After-School Club

*Mentor plan · KS3 (open to all abilities) · runs across the year to the registration deadline (\~May) · 2026–2027*

| What it is | A weekly after-school club where students design, build and enter a real project in the global [Coolest Projects](https://online.coolestprojects.org/) online showcase. It sits alongside — not inside — the taught KS3 curriculum (see `KS3-ICT-Curriculum-v4.md`); the club is where students who want to go further make something of their own. |
| :---- | :---- |
| **The outcome** | By the deadline, each student (solo or in a small team) has a finished project **registered and submitted** to Coolest Projects, with a short video or shared Scratch project and their answers to the three registration questions. |
| **How it's run** | Mentor-supported and project-led, not graded. Students work at their own pace through the Raspberry Pi Foundation's four-stage process, with the mentor coaching rather than teaching from the front. Use the **Mentor Plan** and **Youth Workbook** from the guidance page (below). |
| **Who it's for** | Any KS3 student, any experience level, working solo or in a small team. **Eligibility:** entrants are 18 or under; those **under 13 need an adult to register** on their behalf. Projects/videos are shared publicly with **comments disabled** — so parental consent and uploads are arranged early. |

## **The four-stage journey — the spine of the club**

Coolest Projects is built on four stages. The club moves through them across the year, looping back to test and improve as real makers do. **Download the current Youth Workbook, Stage 1–4 session plans and Mentor Plan from the [Coolest Projects guidance page](https://online.coolestprojects.org/guidance) — always take them fresh so you have the latest version.**

| Stage | What students do |
| :---- | :---- |
| **1 · Generate Ideas** | Decide **who** it's for and **why**; brainstorm (three ideas, "think big"); pick one; define what it **will** and **won't** do; choose the technology and category. |
| **2 · Design & Create** | Plan the user journey; sketch the screens/build; build a **first working prototype** — "just enough that someone can try it." |
| **3 · Test & Debug** | Someone else tries the prototype; note what works and what to fix; **iterate**, then decide the next action. |
| **4 · Present & Register** | Answer the **three registration questions**; finalise a Scratch description **or** record a video showing the project *and the code*; run the checklist; **register and submit**. |

**The three registration questions** (students should be able to answer these well by the end):

1. Tell us about your project. Why did you choose to make it?  
2. What did you find difficult while making it, and how did you work it out?  
3. Is there anything you would do differently, or add, if you had more time?

## **Session roadmap**

Sessions are weekly and flexible — this is the shape across the year, aligned to the Coolest Projects calendar, **not** a fixed lesson count. Confirm the exact 2027 deadline on the [website](https://online.coolestprojects.org/) and work back from it.

| Phase | Roughly when | What happens | Stage |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Launch & ideas** | First few sessions | Watch showcase videos from the [online gallery](https://online.coolestprojects.org/); agree club & AI ground rules; explore the seven categories; **generate and shortlist ideas**; newcomers try an optional skills taster (below) to pick a technology; register a **draft idea** at [rpf.io/coolest](https://rpf.io/coolest). | **1** |
| **Design & build** | The bulk of the year | Design the project, then build a working **prototype** and keep improving it. Mentor coaches, notes who needs support, encourages "build the bit you know first." | **2** |
| **Test & improve** | Ongoing, intensifying mid-year | Regular **user-testing** with other club members; fix the top issues; iterate. Keep a simple build/bug log. | **3** |
| **Present & register** | Weeks before the deadline (\~May) | Answer the three questions; finalise the Scratch description **or** screen-record the project *and code* into a short video; run the registration checklist; **submit**. | **4** |
| **Showcase & celebrate** | After submission | Share projects with the club/school; celebrate everyone who entered — *even an unfinished project counts if the story is told well.* | — |

## **Optional skills tasters — on-ramps for beginners**

Newcomers who don't yet know what to make can try one or two quick tasters early on, then commit to a technology for their own project. These are short and hands-on, not full projects:

- **Scratch / Games** — a small playable game (builds on Y7-extension Scratch). *Simplest registration: shared on the Scratch website, no video needed.*  
- **Web / AI "vibe coding"** — a web page or simple educational simulator built with AI-assisted tools (e.g. Canva AI), learning to read, test and explain the code.  
- **Micro:bit / Hardware** — a physical input→process→output build in MakeCode (builds on Y7 Micro:bit).

Confident students can skip the tasters and go straight to their own project.

## **Registration & showcase**

- **Seven categories:** Scratch · Games · Web · Mobile Apps · Hardware · Advanced Programming · **Artificial Intelligence** (newest). Students pick the one that fits their project, plus up to three topics.  
- **Registration route:** Scratch projects are shared on the Scratch website (no video). **All other categories need a short video** showing the project *and the code*, uploaded to YouTube/Vimeo (comments off, link public).  
- **Checklist before submitting:** one project per entrant; presented in English; video shows the code (non-Scratch); comments disabled; link is public and viewable by anyone.  
- **Deadline:** register/submit by \~May (targeting Coolest Projects Online 2027\) — **confirm the exact date on the website**.

## **Safeguarding & logistics**

- **Parental consent** for public sharing, and for uploading video to YouTube/Vimeo — arrange **early**. For under-13s, a parent/guardian uploads and registers on the student's behalf.  
- **Public sharing with comments disabled**, links not private.  
- **Accounts & kit:** Scratch and any AI-tool accounts set up in advance; Micro:bit kit and Raspberry Pi devices where used; work saved to OneDrive so nothing is lost across a long project.  
- **AI-tools rule:** the AI is the assistant, the student is the author — they must understand and be able to explain their own code (the video must show it).
